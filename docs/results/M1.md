# M1: tensor-core GEMM

*Flint: GPT-2 from scratch on hand-written CUDA kernels.*

M1 built the matrix multiplies GPT-2's training step needs: FP16 tensor-core
GEMMs with FP32 accumulation in the three layouts of a linear layer, plus four
fused epilogues. All of it is measured against cuBLAS and cuBLASLt on an RTX 2080
Ti (Turing, sm_75) and an RTX 4050 Laptop GPU (Ada, sm_89). On the 2080 Ti every
layout beats its target: the forward GEMM runs at 82-89% of cuBLAS, the input
gradient at 86-99%, and the weight gradient at 91-108%.

## What M1 delivered

- **Seven GEMM functions** in `include/flint/flint.h`. Every matrix is row-major,
  contiguous FP16, and every product accumulates in FP32:

  | Function | Computes | In GPT-2 |
  | --- | --- | --- |
  | `flint_gemm_nt` | y = x wᵀ, FP16 y | the forward pass of a linear layer |
  | `flint_gemm_nn` | y = x w, FP16 y | the input gradient, dX = dY W |
  | `flint_gemm_tn` | y = xᵀ w (or y += xᵀ w), FP32 y | the weight gradient, dW = dYᵀ X, accumulated across micro-batches |
  | `flint_gemm_nt_bias` | y = x wᵀ + bias | linear layers with a bias |
  | `flint_gemm_nt_bias_gelu` | pre = x wᵀ + bias, y = GELU(pre) | the MLP's up projection |
  | `flint_gemm_nn_dgelu` | y = (x w) ⊙ GELU′(pre) | the MLP down projection's input gradient, carried back through GELU |
  | `flint_gemm_nt_bias_residual` | y = residual + (x wᵀ + bias), FP32 | the two projections that write the residual stream |

  Each output rounds once, from the FP32 sum with its epilogue applied. GELU is
  GPT-2's tanh form, computed in FP32. Results are bit-identical from run to run
  on a given GPU. k is capped at 65,536, because the tensor cores' FP32
  accumulation truncates and the error grows with k.
- **One kernel body for every layout and epilogue.** Each block computes a
  128x128 tile of y with 8 warps of 64x32, using `ldmatrix` and `mma.sync`
  m16n8k8 (FP16 in, FP32 accumulate), one 32-wide step of k at a time through a
  single padded shared-memory stage. Blocks take tiles in groups of 8 rows for
  L2 reuse. The weight-gradient GEMM splits k when its output has few tiles,
  and sums the splits in a fixed order. The same code runs on both GPUs; it
  uses 126-128 registers, so two blocks fit on an SM.
- **Correctness:** against a PyTorch FP32 reference, Flint's error must be within
  2x that of PyTorch's own FP16 path for outputs and 3x for gradients, on every
  GPT-2 shape, odd shapes, outlier channels and inputs near FP16's range. Exact
  tests pin the rounding and the order of the adds, and the whole suite also
  runs under compute-sanitizer's four tools. At the benchmarked shapes, the NT,
  NN and residual GEMMs match cuBLAS bit for bit.
- **Benchmarks and profiling:** `flint_bench gemm` times every layout and
  epilogue against its baseline, and four scripts profile the gap to cuBLAS
  (`scripts/profile_gemm.py`, `nsys_kernels.py`, `k_sweep.py`, `sass_loop.py`).

## Method

The protocol is M0's ([M0.md](M0.md#method)): identical random inputs rotated
past 4x the L2 cache, a 1.5 s warm-up, samples of at least 100 ms, and 10 valid
rounds that alternate Flint and the baseline, with NVML monitoring the clocks,
power and other processes. On top of it:

- **Shapes:** GPT-2 small's at 8,192 tokens. Forward (NT, MxNxK): qkv
  8192x2304x768, attention projection 8192x768x768, MLP up 8192x3072x768, MLP
  down 8192x768x3072, logits 8192x50304x768. The input-gradient (NN) and
  weight-gradient (TN) shapes are the same layers' backward multiplies; TN is
  timed accumulating into y.
- **Baselines:** `cublasGemmEx` with FP32 compute and its default algorithm,
  with FP16 split-K reductions disallowed, as PyTorch's FP16 path sets it. The
  fused kernels run against cuBLASLt's matching epilogue, timing every
  candidate its heuristic offers and using the fastest. cuBLASLt rejects an
  FP32 bias with FP16 output, so the bias baselines read the bias rounded to
  FP16, as PyTorch's autocast does.
- **FLOPs** are 2mnk. The 2080 Ti's rated peak is 512 FLOP per SM per clock
  at the reference 1,545 MHz boost, 53.8 TFLOPS. "Clock-adj." is the fraction
  of the peak at the mean SM clock NVML logged during Flint's samples. The
  laptop has no single rated peak.
- **Output checks:** each case compares Flint's output with the baseline's
  after timing, and checks that Flint gives the same bits twice.

The tables come from the raw logs through `scripts/results_table.py`.

## Where the remaining gap comes from

The 2080 Ti has no performance counters, so the explanation rests on different
evidence on each GPU, and each claim below says which:

- **[2080 Ti, nsys]** Nsight Systems traces of every benchmarked case: cuBLAS's
  kernel names and launch configurations.
- **[2080 Ti, timing]** A sweep of time against k at fixed m and n, fitted per
  side in time and in SM cycles (time times NVML's clock).
- **[both, static SASS]** Flint's own compiled main loops (cuBLAS is never
  disassembled).
- **[laptop, ncu]** Nsight Compute counters for Flint and cuBLAS at all 15 plain
  shapes.

The raw data is in [`raw/M1/profile/`](raw/M1/profile/), from commit `f1a308a`,
whose kernels are identical to `a0c930d`'s.

**Not the instruction, not the waves** [2080 Ti, nsys]. cuBLAS uses the same
m16n8k8 instruction (its `s1688` kernels). For the forward shapes it runs
128x128 tiles with 128 threads (n = 2,304 and 3,072) or 256x128 tiles with 256
threads (n = 768), at 2 and 1 blocks per SM. Its tile counts fill the same
8.47 and 2.82 waves of the 2080 Ti's 68 SMs as Flint's 128x128 tiles at 2 blocks
per SM.

**The gap splits four ways** [2080 Ti, timing]. Each side's time and SM cycles
were fitted against k = 768-6,144, only over the k where cuBLAS runs one kernel
(it switches tiles as k grows; nsys checked every point). Main loop = the
difference in cycles per unit of k, times k, at Flint's clock; fixed = the
difference in cycles at k = 0, ± its standard error; clock = what Flint's lower
SM clock costs, 0.2-2.6% below cuBLAS's under the power cap. Ratios and gaps
are from the sweep's own runs ([`k-sweep-rtx-2080-ti.json`](raw/M1/profile/k-sweep-rtx-2080-ti.json)),
in µs:

| Shape | Ratio | Gap | Main loop | Fixed | Clock | Rest |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| NT 8192x2304x768 (qkv) | 0.821 | 116 | 38 | 27 ± 16 | 1 | 51 |
| NT 8192x768x768 | 0.862 | 31 | 12 | 23 ± 8 | 3 | -7 |
| NT 8192x768x3072 | 0.881 | 97 | 50 | 23 ± 8 | 14 | 10 |
| NN 8192x768x768 | 0.865 | 30 | 7 | 22 ± 1 | 2 | -1 |

| NN 8192x3072x768 | 0.906 | 79 | 26 | 42 ± 3 | 12 | -1 |

- Measured in cycles, Flint's main loop runs at 0.934-0.935 of cuBLAS's rate in
  NT and 0.960-0.964 in NN.
- At k = 768 the fixed cost is the largest part of the gap; at k = 3,072 the
  main loop is.
- qkv's "rest" is cuBLAS's kernel choice: at k = 768 it runs its 128x128
  kernel, 56 µs faster than its 256x128 kernel's fit (from k = 1,536-6,144)
  predicts.

**Why the main loop is slower.**
- [both, static SASS] Each 32-wide step of k compiles to a barrier, 4 global
  loads, 4 shared-memory stores, a second barrier, then 12 `ldmatrix` and 64
  HMMA. The compiler moved the loads below the first barrier, and each load's
  first use is its store, 14-26 static cycles later on sm_75. So a block can't overlap
  its next tile's loads with its own math; only the other block on the SM can.
- [2080 Ti, nsys] cuBLAS's kernels hold 0.033 MB (128x128) or 0.065 MB
  (256x128) of shared memory against Flint's 0.020 MB, which is consistent with
  double buffering. That's an inference: cuBLAS's code isn't examined.
- [laptop, ncu] What it costs on sm_89, at the same HMMA count:
  - cuBLAS takes 0.852-0.899 of Flint's elapsed SM cycles in NT and
    0.871-0.894 in NN. Flint's tensor pipe is active 41-44% of ncu's peak
    against cuBLAS's 48-50%. Every cuBLAS kernel stops near 50%, so that is
    probably the FP32-accumulate ceiling.
  - Waiting for global loads takes 16% of a Flint warp's time in NT, against
    about 1% for cuBLAS; barrier waits take 9%, against 2-6%.
  - Flint executes 1.25-1.36x cuBLAS's instructions in NT and 1.34-1.46x its
    shared-memory load traffic. Each of its warps computes 64x32, against an
    inferred 64x64 for cuBLAS's 4-warp kernel, so it loads more fragments per
    HMMA.
  - The tile stores' 80-byte padded rows conflict 2-way: 50% of NT's
    shared-store wavefronts are excess, and 33% of NN's.
  - Issue slots are only 11-13% busy, so the GEMM isn't limited by
    instruction issue. Switching sm_89 to m16n8k16 wouldn't help, and it stays
    on m16n8k8.

**The fixed cost isn't attributed on the 2080 Ti.** The epilogue is a candidate:
it stores y as 32 four-byte stores per thread per tile [both, static SASS], twice
cuBLAS's store instructions on the laptop. There, the code after the main loop
holds 3.2-3.7% of Flint's stall samples at k = 768 and about 1% at 3,072
[laptop, ncu].

**TN** [2080 Ti, nsys]. At 3072x768 and 768x3072, Flint's GEMM kernel is faster
than cuBLAS's split-K kernel (835 and 823 µs against 844 and 842); its separate
reduction, 86 µs, is the whole gap. At 768x768 Flint is ahead; cuBLAS's kernel
there runs a grid 5 deep, which reads as a 5-way split of k.

**Fused baselines** [2080 Ti, nsys]. cuBLASLt's bias and residual epilogues run
inside its GEMM kernel. Its GELU_AUX_BIAS and DGELU run the plain GEMM, then a
separate epilogue kernel: 690 + 327 µs and 703 + 654 µs, against Flint's fused
946 and 880 µs.

## Limitations

- **The laptop's numbers are indicative.** Desktop applications always use its
  GPU, so its runs need `--allow-busy`, and its clocks and power vary from run to
  run. Even the `mma` loop reaches only 94% of the peak at NVML's mean clock,
  which suggests NVML's clock doesn't track its effective clock. Headline numbers
  come from the 2080 Ti.
- **No locked clocks on the 2080 Ti.** The server has no root, so clocks are
  logged, not locked, and the GEMMs run power-capped.
- **k is capped at 65,536**, where Flint's error is 1.1x the peer's. FP32
  accumulation on the tensor cores truncates, and at 2^18 the error would fail
  the gate.
- **The residual kernel needs an even n** (GPT-2's is 768): it reads and
  writes FP32 pairs, and every formulation with a path for odd n slowed its
  main loop on the 2080 Ti.
- **Bitwise reproducibility is per GPU.** The weight gradient's split count
  depends on the SM count, so the 2080 Ti and the laptop can sum in different
  orders.
- **Baselines.** The bias baselines read an FP16 bias (up to 2.6e-4 relative L2
  from Flint's FP32 bias). PyTorch's own path for GELU's backward, cuBLAS plus a
  separate kernel, isn't measured; it may be faster than cuBLASLt's DGELU on
  Turing.
- **Open questions from the profile:**
  - Which part of the main loop's gap on the 2080 Ti is exposed latency and
    which is shared-memory traffic.
  - What the fixed cost per call is.
  - Why NT 8192x2304x12288, outside GPT-2's shapes, dips to 83.9% of cuBLAS
    with rounds from 75% to 89%, while k = 8,192 and 16,384 run at 92.4% and
    88.5% ([log](raw/M1/profile/sweep/bench-rtx-2080-ti-largek-nt-8192x2304.json)).
- **Untested corners:** no test needs a matrix of 4 GiB or more, so the 64-bit
  offsets those would use are untested.
